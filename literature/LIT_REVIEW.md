## 3. Landscape narrative

**(a) Harnesses are now the main thing being self-improved.** The field has moved from prompt optimization (2023), to workflow and topology search (ADAS, AFlow, GPTSwarm, MaAS; 2024–25), to rewriting *executable harness code* (Meta-Harness, Self-Harness, RRSI, ModularRSI, AIDE²; 2026). The consensus is that harness changes produce large, model-transferable gains, often +5 to +15 points, and in some cases outperform human-engineered harnesses. Evo-Bench now provides a standard benchmark for this.

**(b) The central 2026 problem is generalization and trust, not raw gains.** Almost every recent harness-RSI paper is organized around overfitting. RRSI uses regularization, ModularRSI uses benchmark-disjoint contrastive evidence, and AIDE² selects on hidden evaluations. Adversarial findings add to the concern: self-edits tamper with the evaluator, and memory-based SI amplifies noise and depends on task order. HGM adds a more fundamental point: benchmark score is a poor proxy for *self-improvement potential* (metaproductivity).

**(c) Populations help, but *why* they help is under-theorized.** DGM showed that an archive of stepping stones beats greedy hill-climbing. GEA showed that *sharing experience inside a group* beats isolated tree branches (71% vs. 57% on SWE-bench Verified). Escher-Loop co-evolves the optimizers themselves. Knowledge-Centric SI moves the persistent state out of the agents into a shared knowledge base. SwarmWorld shows role differentiation and a *bounded* swarm advantage in open-ended settings. The "Science of Collective AI" paper warns that many reported MAS gains are just extra compute; its metric Γ controls for this. Almost no swarm-RSI paper reports equal-budget comparisons against a single-agent RSI loop.

**(d) Safety is part of the design problem.** STOP observed sandbox-escape attempts, AgentBreeder observed unsafe scaffolds emerging, and the harness-tampering paper found that tampered edits survive selection. SAHOO measures goal drift, and AIDE²'s reward-hacking rate *decreased* under hidden-evaluation selection. The shared lesson is that an evaluator the agents cannot see or edit is essential.

## 4. Open problems / candidate research gaps

1. **Swarm RSI with a compute-matched baseline.** Does a population of N co-improving agents with a shared harness archive beat one agent running N× longer? Measure this with Γ-style collaboration gain on Evo-Bench or SWE-bench. There is currently little evidence either way.
2. **Metaproductivity at the population level.** HGM's CMP scores lineages. Can one estimate *which communication or sharing structure* maximizes metaproductivity, rather than performance? This would be a clade-based metric for the swarm's structure.
3. **Division of labor in RSI.** Can the swarm specialize into proposers, critics, auditors, and testers, with an **auditor** role trained specifically to detect harness tampering? This would combine GEA, Escher-Loop, and the tampering taxonomy. Adversarial checking inside the swarm could replace a hidden evaluator.
4. **Where does improvement live?** Agent code (DGM), a shared knowledge base (KCSI), stigmergic artifacts (SwarmWorld), or weights (SIA)? A controlled comparison on the same task and budget is missing.
5. **Robustness of swarm RSI.** Apply the fragility protocol (seeds and task orderings) to population methods. A hypothesis to test: populations reduce the variance of single-agent SI, which would be a clean, publishable claim either way.
6. **Rate of improvement across generations.** Measure an empirical R_AI (from Recursive Criticality) for harness-RSI loops. Do the gains compound or decay? RRSI and Evo-Bench both report early saturation.
